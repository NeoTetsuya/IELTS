Modifying Nouns with Adjectives

Adjectives describe or modify nouns. They appear in two main patterns:

After the verb 'be'

Modifies the subject directly.

The desert is hot. modifies
The foxes are small. modifies

Before a noun

Placed directly in front of the noun.

It's an interesting article. modifies
He is a tall man. modifies

Core Rules

  • Invariant Form: Adjectives NEVER change form for plural nouns.
    The rooms are big. | The rooms are bigs.
  • Indefinite Articles (a/an): Use "a" before consonant sounds (a good student). Use "an" before vowel sounds (an excellent teacher).
2

Adjectives with How and What Questions

Use these structures to ask for descriptions, measurements, or conditions:

How + Adjective

Inquire about measurements.

  • How old are you? (Age)
  • How tall are you? (Height)
How + Be

Ask about general conditions.

  • How's the weather today?
What + Be + Subj + Like

Request a general description.

  • What's John like? (He's funny)
What kind of + Noun

Ask about specific types.

  • What kind of photographer is he?
3

Using Nouns as Adjectives

Nouns can be placed before other nouns to modify or describe them.

Singular Form Rule

The modifying noun MUST remain singular.

horse trainer
horses trainer

Nouns Ending in -ing

camping trip
shopping center

Common Examples (Try hovering!)

desert sand
pencil case
baby elephant
weather report
